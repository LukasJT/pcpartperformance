6. BUILD A REAL MORNING PRICE SYSTEM

Once-per-morning checking is acceptable. Build a shared backend service for components, shop listings, builder totals and prebuilts.

Inspect and extend the existing offer schema and validation. Use authorized retailer APIs/feeds first, or permitted access to exact product pages where suitable. Research actual access requirements and reuse configured credentials safely.

Investigate Best Buy's official product API as one starting point; verify its permissions and regional coverage. Do not assume a US API covers Canada or that every store exposes the same data.

If PCPartPicker access is authorized and technically available, evaluate it as an adapter. Do not make the whole service depend on an unofficial scraper or bypass Cloudflare protections.

Every observation must identify the exact product variant, retailer SKU, merchant/seller, condition, country, currency, direct listing URL, observed price, availability evidence, checkedAt and expiry. Unknown stock remains unknown.

Use the existing Cloudflare Worker where practical. Add durable storage, a tested scheduled handler and a morning refresh around 7:00 a.m. America/Toronto, correctly accounting for UTC schedules and daylight saving. Make runs idempotent and bounded.

Use a 15-minute response cache and request deduplication where appropriate. A cache duration does not make a once-daily observation live. Keep daily checked snapshots separate from any short-lived current-price policy; do not silently weaken existing expiry rules.

Display concise timestamps such as "Checked today at 7:03 a.m." and explain excluded tax/shipping. Failed checks must not advance the last successful timestamp. Show stale or unavailable states honestly.

Rank the lowest eligible offer among checked stores, not the "cheapest everywhere." Keep CAD and USD explicit behind the existing country control. Recalculate quantities, owned parts, partial totals and missing prices correctly.

Add timeouts, rate limits, bounded retries, backoff, parser-change detection and per-source health. Keep secrets server-side; constrain upstream URLs and validate responses. Do not fetch arbitrary user URLs.

Keep real observation history if permitted. Never synthesize historical prices, discounts, stock or "lowest ever" claims.

Prepare a dry run, request/cost estimate and source-access checklist. Activate the schedule only after the relevant access and release authorization are established. Verify a real deployed execution before describing it as running.
