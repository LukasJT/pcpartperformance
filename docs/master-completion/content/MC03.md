# Choose a PC power supply: wattage, connectors, fit and the correct cables

A wattage label is one requirement, not a complete PSU selection. Check the whole system, exact component connection instructions, physical layout and manufacturer-approved modular cables. Preserve unknowns; matching plugs or a plausible 3D scene do not establish a safe installation.

## Keep different power quantities separate

A graphics card’s typical board power, a processor’s manufacturer power fields, a PSU’s rated output and measured wall consumption describe different quantities. For example, AMD lists 220 W typical board power for the RX 9070 and 304 W for the RX 9070 XT, with separate minimum PSU recommendations. Those are design specifications, not a measured total for your PC.

Collect the requirements for the exact selected components and intended configuration. Do not add mismatched labels and declare the result a verified system measurement. If an application needs an actual energy or operating-cost observation, use a documented measurement and the electricity planner with its assumptions visible. A shopping calculator must not silently become electrical testing.

Sources: [AMD — Radeon RX 9070 specifications](https://www.amd.com/en/products/graphics/desktops/radeon/9000-series/amd-radeon-rx-9070.html); [AMD — Radeon RX 9070 XT specifications](https://www.amd.com/en/products/graphics/desktops/radeon/9000-series/amd-radeon-rx-9070xt.html)

## Inventory every required connection

List the motherboard main and CPU power requirements, graphics-card connections, storage, powered hubs and other applicable devices. Read exact instructions, including any supplied adapter and its stated conditions. Count the supported connections rather than assuming that one available connector proves the whole arrangement.

A missing connection and an undocumented connection are different states. Resolve the latter through manufacturer instructions or support before buying adapters around a guess. Never treat a PCIe or CPU-power plug as interchangeable just because a similar housing can be pushed into place. The useful record ties each required device connection to the exact supply and approved cable.

## Treat modular cables as exact PSU accessories

CORSAIR’s compatibility chart explicitly warns that Type 3 and Type 4 modular cables are not compatible with Type 5 supplies and that incorrect cables can damage hardware. Those type names describe CORSAIR’s documented families; they are not a cross-brand standard. Use the chart and exact PSU/revision rather than a generic cable color or familiar connector shape.

Keep the supply’s original approved cables with its identity. If buying a used PSU or replacing a missing cable, verify the actual cable part through the manufacturer before installation. A listing that says “fits most modular PSUs” is not equivalent evidence. Do not carry the old cable set forward into a replacement supply unless exact compatibility is documented.

Sources: [CORSAIR — PSU cable compatibility chart](https://www.corsair.com/us/en/s/psu-cable-compatibility); [CORSAIR — PSU cable compatibility explainer](https://www.corsair.com/us/en/explorer/diy-builder/power-supply-units/psu-cable-compatibility/)

## Check where the supply and connected cables will fit

Read the case’s PSU form-factor and length conditions for the installed layout. Drive cages, supports and cable routing can affect the usable arrangement. Fractal’s original North sheet, for example, presents configuration-dependent component limits; another North edition needs its own document. A case name alone is insufficient.

Keep cable access and routing with the body dimensions. A PSU may meet one length comparison while leaving an unresolved installation or connection question. Use the builder’s visual preview to understand the general arrangement, but retain documentary measurements and manual checks. Do not invent a universal clearance buffer or imply that the model certifies the fit.

Sources: [Fractal Design — Original North English product sheet](https://www.fractal-design.com/app/uploads/2022/12/North_Product-Sheet_EN.pdf)

## Consider reuse as a supported condition, not a default

An existing supply is an owned asset, not automatically a suitable or unsuitable one. Record its exact model, condition as known, approved cable inventory and requirements for the next configuration. A larger number on a new box does not by itself demonstrate that replacing the working unit solves a problem.

If essential identity, connection or condition evidence is missing, mark it for verification or obtain qualified assistance. Do not open a PSU to investigate it. For a used purchase, missing cables and uncertain history are part of the decision and cost comparison. This article does not remotely diagnose a failing supply or predict its remaining service life.

## Make the complete decision record before ordering

Use the worksheet to compare candidates on the same requirements. Add exact region-specific prices only when documented, including required approved cables, delivery and other exclusions. Do not conceal a missing accessory price inside a partial subtotal. A manufacturer specification, a current seller quote and a measured performance review are different evidence types.

Save the selected supply and outstanding checks with the complete build. Confirm that a later GPU, case or drive-layout change does not inherit an old verdict. The result should be a connection and installation plan someone can understand, not a claim of “100% compatible” based solely on wattage. No PSU testing, noise rating or current best-price ranking was performed for this guide.

**Original PSU selection checklist**

| Question | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- |
| Power requirement | Exact component/system instructions and stated use |
| Connectors | Required devices and approved connection route |
| Modular cable match | Exact manufacturer chart and cable part |
| Physical installation | Case layout, supply dimensions and routing |
| Reuse or used purchase | Known identity, condition and cable inventory |
| Complete cost | Documented regional quote and required extras |
| Remaining uncertainty | Specific source or support action to resolve it |

## Audit reuse in three separate rows

Separate the power-supply unit, its DC cable set and the installation accessories. Keeping the unit does not prove that every cable in a drawer belongs to it. Replacing the unit does not make the existing routed cables compatible with the replacement. Seasonic warns that modular wiring can differ across brands and between models within a brand. Identify the supplied or explicitly approved set before connecting it.

NZXT provides a current concrete example: its Gold Core supplies are not compatible with cables from the previous C-series generations despite connectors that can physically mate. This is why “same brand, same wattage” is not a usable cable approval. The exact manufacturer record must connect the particular cable and supply. Do not infer approval from a connector photograph or try to create it by rewiring the cable.

**Reuse is three decisions, not one wattage comparison**

| Item | Keep only after checking | If unresolved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PSU unit | Exact identity, known condition, intended load and required supported connections | Obtain model-specific help; do not open the enclosure |
| Modular DC cables | Supplied set or explicit compatibility with this exact PSU/revision | Do not connect an unidentified cable |
| Mounting and routing | Case support, cable reach and the intended drive/fan arrangement | Retain a fit or accessory check and any missing cost |

Sources: [Seasonic — Which cables are compatible with my power supply?](https://knowledge.seasonic.com/article/68-cable-compatibility); [NZXT — Never reuse PSU cables: why modular cables are not interchangeable](https://nzxt.com/en-intl/blogs/news/psu-cable-compatibility)

## Include the cost of making reuse possible

Compare two complete plans: retain the identified working supply with any documented accessories it needs, or replace it with an identified unit and the correct included cables. Record the actual seller quote, region and exclusions for every purchase. A supply already owned is not new spending, but an unpriced replacement cable is not free.

If a necessary approved replacement is unavailable, the reuse route remains unresolved. Keep its cost and availability question visible rather than marking the overall build ready. Recheck after a graphics-card, case or storage change. This workflow helps organize a purchasing decision; it cannot inspect the electrical condition of a used supply remotely.
