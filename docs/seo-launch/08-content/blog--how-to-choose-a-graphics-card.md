# How to choose a graphics card for your PC

URL: /blog/how-to-choose-a-graphics-card/
Meta description: Choose a GPU around your games, monitor and exact card dimensions. Understand VRAM, benchmark settings, power connections and total upgrade cost.
By PC Part Performance. Materially revised October 3, 2026. Original publication date not recorded.

Start with your games and monitor, then check comparable game tests and the dimensions of the exact card. A GPU chip name, memory capacity or generated-frame headline cannot answer all three questions.

## Set a useful target before shopping

Write down the games you actually play, your monitor resolution and refresh rate, and the settings you would accept. A competitive shooter at reduced detail poses a different question from a demanding single-player game with ray tracing. Make the target specific enough that a review can answer it.

Include the rest of the system in your plan. An old processor, a frame-rate cap or a monitor running at the wrong refresh rate can make a GPU upgrade disappointing. Compare the same game at different settings on your existing PC before assuming the graphics card is the only limit.

## Read benchmarks with their settings attached

Look for original testing that identifies the test PC, game version, resolution and graphics preset. Compare native rendering separately from upscaling and frame generation. Average FPS describes one aspect of performance; frame-time consistency and clearly defined low-percentile results add useful context.

Do not merge numbers from different reviews into a single precise ranking. A review suite is a sample of games, not a universal score for everything you might play. Our FPS tool is a model estimate; use it to explore a setup, then confirm your shortlist against published testing.

## Treat VRAM as capacity, not a performance score

The RTX 5070 has 12 GB of GDDR7; the RTX 5060 Ti discussed in our comparison has 16 GB. That capacity difference does not establish which GPU renders a game faster. Texture settings and workload demands determine whether memory capacity becomes a constraint.

Check that a retailer listing specifies the right capacity. The RTX 5060 Ti also exists in an 8 GB version. A photo or shortened listing title can conceal a variant difference, so copy the full model number into your purchase checklist.

## Check the complete card and power path

A board partner can fit the same GPU chip to different coolers. Check card length, height, occupied slots and connector clearance against the case manual and your planned radiator layout. A photograph is useful for identifying the cooler, but cannot replace a measurement.

Check the PSU’s actual connectors and the card maker’s instructions as well as wattage. Our builder organizes indexed checks and models; it does not certify cable clearance or every mounting point. Leave unresolved measurements on the checklist until you have documentary evidence.

## Compare the whole upgrade cost

Compare exact, in-stock listings in the country where you will buy. Add shipping, tax and any required PSU, case or monitor change. An apparently cheaper card can cost more as a complete upgrade. Retailer search links on this site are navigation aids, not a claim that a specific offer is available.

Keep your shortlist small: two or three cards that meet the target, with known fit and a purchase route. Recheck prices when you are ready to order rather than treating a launch price or an old review price as today’s quote.
